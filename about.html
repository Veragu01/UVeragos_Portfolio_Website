About Me

I’m in my final year of studying Computer Science at Unitec and expect to finish my degree in June 2027. During my time at Unitec, I’ve explored many areas of software development. I’ve built full stack web applications with Django and React, and had the chance to develop using a built-in AI agent. I’ve also created desktop applications in C# with .NET Framework and in Java, including database management tools built with Windows Forms and a proof-of-concept D&D character sheet manager using Java Swing. I’ve studied mobile app development with Java and XML in Android Studio, too. For one project, I created an app concept for a fictional gardening business, with features for customers, gardeners, and administrators. I also built apps that interacted with live databases and APIs. Alongside the practical development work, I’ve learned how to collaborate on projects using GitHub and its many tools, and gained experience with SDLC phases, development methodologies, and project management software such as Jira.

Outside of uni, I’ve worked on several projects of my own. You’ll find some of them on this website, along with posts about what I built and how I went about it. I’ve also had two roles that have given me experience teaching and working with people. Since my second semester, I’ve been a PASS Leader (teaching assistant) for diploma classes I’d previously completed. In this role I spend 4–8 hours each week helping students work through topics and exercises they find difficult. An important part of this role is to help them learn when they didn't understand it the first time. This means re-teaching content in a different manner rather than showing/telling them the answer. I’ve been doing this for four semesters now, and I’ve been asked to continue until I graduate next semester. More recently, during the July 2026 school holidays, a librarian at New Lynn Library asked if I’d be interested in teaching kids to code. I loved the idea as I already had experience teaching programming, and I have two kids of my own whom I’d love to teach one day. The classes have 15 kids aged 7–11, so I asked a friend from my course to help. Since then, we have since run 5 workshops teaching kids how to create games with various features using Scratch. The games are simple and reminiscent of 2010 era web flash games, with the intention of spiking an interest in programming or game development in the kids.

Before studying, I had a successful career as an Architectural Designer. I prepared drawing sets for council consent, met with clients throughout the process from the initial intake onwards, and managed site progress. That experience taught me a lot about real-world projects, and many of those lessons carry over to software development. After I graduate, I plan to pursue a career in software development. I find it incredibly rewarding, and I enjoy always having something new to learn. I haven’t settled on a particular area yet, so I’m keeping my options open and seeing where things lead.

Outside of study and work I’m a part-time single parent to two beautiful, hilarious kids. I go to the gym, play D&D with friends, enjoy video games, and have fallen down the self-hosting rabbit hole by running my own Linux server at home. On this website, you’ll find my contact information, resume, and personal projects, both finished and in progress. Feel free to get in touch, whether you’d just like to talk about a project you’ve seen here or you’re a prospective employer. I’d love to hear from you.
